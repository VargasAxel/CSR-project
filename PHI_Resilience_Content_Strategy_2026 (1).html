Executive Summary

At a glance.

This 12-month content strategy positions PHI Resilience as the leading voice in proactive, resilience-grounded Corporate Social Responsibility across the Philippines. It delivers simultaneously on three business goals — lead generation, thought leadership, and organic search traffic — across three priority audience segments: Multinational Corporations, NGOs and Foundations, and Donor and Bilateral Agencies.

The strategy is anchored by the proprietary Periodic Table of Basic CSR framework, which provides both intellectual differentiation and natural content architecture. Every content piece maps to one of six element groups, creating a self-reinforcing internal linking structure that compounds authority over time.

I
Part One

Strategic foundation.

Business Context

PHI Resilience is a Philippines-based CSR and sustainability consultancy that transforms Corporate Social Responsibility from reactive charity into proactive, ESG-aligned, measurable action. The firm offers six integrated service lines and is differentiated by its proprietary Periodic Table of Basic CSR framework — a modular structure that categorizes every essential CSR service into six element groups.

The leadership team brings a rare combination of 25+ years of CSR and ESG strategy experience, registered social work credentials, grassroots community development expertise, and direct multinational corporate partnership track record. This interdisciplinary blend is a material competitive advantage that should be foregrounded in content.

The Three Goals — Why Balance Matters

You have elected to pursue all three content goals in parallel rather than sequencing them. This is the correct approach for a consultancy at PHI Resilience's stage because the three goals reinforce rather than compete with one another. Organic search traffic feeds the top of the funnel with relevant prospects. Thought leadership content converts those prospects' interest into credibility and trust. Lead generation assets capture contact information and convert interest into actual conversations.

A pure SEO strategy would generate traffic but not close deals with sophisticated donor agencies or MNC sustainability directors, who respond to credibility more than keyword optimization. The three-way balance serves the full buyer journey.

Competitive Positioning

The principal regional competitor benchmark is bernardbc.com (Malaysia), which has built strong authority through 130+ articles on Malaysian ESG regulations, carbon tax, ISO certifications, and NSRF compliance. Bernard's content engine produces high volumes of technical SEO content but is geographically limited to Malaysia and does not cover disaster resilience, community development, or the uniquely Philippine intersection of CSR with typhoon risk.

PHI Resilience's defensible content positioning is the intersection of four themes that no competitor in Southeast Asia is currently owning: Philippine-specific CSR practice, disaster and typhoon resilience planning, grassroots community development methodology, and proactive pre-positioning of CSR resources. This is a genuinely uncontested content territory.

The 30-Minute Interview System

The critical mechanism that makes this model work is the 30-minute interview. Twice a month, the outsourced writer schedules a recorded call with either Mardie or Delia. The writer arrives with specific questions on a pre-selected topic. The founder answers conversationally for 20–30 minutes. The writer then produces a structured 1,000-word article from the transcript. The founder reviews and edits in 15 minutes.

This converts founder time — the most expensive and scarcest resource — into published content at a ratio of approximately 30 minutes of founder time per published article. Dramatically more efficient than any other production method, and far more voice-authentic than writer-only content.

Internal Linking Rules
  1. Every blog article must link to at least 2 other PHI Resilience articles in the body copy (not just the footer).
  2. Every hub article must link to all its spoke articles.
  3. Every spoke article must link back to its hub and to at least one other spoke in the same cluster.
  4. Cross-pillar links should use natural transitions — not forced "related reading" callouts.
  5. Every article must end with a contextual CTA: the Periodic Table flagship, the relevant lead magnet, or a specific service page.

Part Nine

Lead magnets.

Gated resources are the single most important lead generation mechanism in a B2B content strategy. Visitors who download a gated resource are three to ten times more likely to become qualified leads than those who only read articles. Three gated resources are recommended for Year 1.

01
Launch Q1 · Audience: MNC CSR
The CSR Readiness Checklist
Format 8-page PDF with 25 diagnostic questions across the six Periodic Table element groups, plus a 1–10 scoring system and interpretation guide.
Why it works Self-diagnosis tools have consistently strong conversion rates. They flatter the downloader's seriousness while exposing them to PHI's proprietary framework as part of the experience.
02
Launch Q3 · Audience: NGO & Ops
DRRM Plan Template — PH Operations
Format 12-page editable Word/PDF with section templates for hazard assessment, continuity protocols, early action triggers, communications plans, and recovery benchmarks.
Why it works Templates have the highest perceived value-to-effort ratio. Downloaders use them directly in work and experience PHI Resilience's methodology while solving their own problem.
03
Launch Q4 · Audience: Sustainability Directors
Annual CSR Impact Report Writing Guide
Format 15-page PDF covering report structure, SDG alignment framing, impact measurement methods, audit-readiness checklist, and a recommended content outline with 3 sample excerpts.
Why it works Year-end annual reporting is a recurring, high-pressure need for every corporate sustainability function. This resource generates leads annually around October–November.

Every month, the content coordinator produces a one-page scorecard tracking each KPI against trajectory. The scorecard is reviewed by Mardie and Delia in a 30-minute monthly meeting. First 90 days.

Strategies fail in implementation, not design. The plan below sequences the first 90 days to maximize the probability of sustainable momentum. If the first three months go well, the remaining nine largely take care of themselves.

01
Infrastructure

  • Publish the merged company profile website and connect Google Search Console + Google Analytics
  • Set up Mailchimp or Brevo; create newsletter template; integrate with WordPress for subscription forms
  • Hire the outsourced writer and the content coordinator/VA; brief them on framework, voice, and audience
  • Publish cornerstone flagship article (The Periodic Table of Basic CSR) and first SEO article (CSR vs ESG vs sustainability)
  • Launch CSR Readiness Checklist gated download with landing page and 3-email nurture sequence

  • Issue the first monthly newsletter to existing contacts plus new subscribers from Month 1
  • First case study research begins — identify a willing client, schedule interview
  • LinkedIn Company Page active with 4–6 posts per week (articles, quotes, visual excerpts, external engagement)
03
Depth

  • Publish 2 blog articles (BCP for Typhoon Operations, Board-level CSR Presentation)

  • First quarterly review: assess KPIs against trajectory, confirm Q2 calendar, adjust if needed

90-Day Success Criteria

By day 90, PHI Resilience should have: six published articles generating early organic traffic (at least 1–2 appearing in Google search for secondary keywords), an active newsletter with 50–100 subscribers, active LinkedIn presences from both founders, the first gated lead magnet live and generating 10–20 downloads, first case study in production, and the full content engine operating without friction.

If all six milestones are hit by day 90, the strategy is on trajectory and the remaining nine months should deliver the Year 1 targets. If fewer than four are hit, the production model needs review — typically by either increasing the outsourced writer's hours or resetting founder time expectations.
